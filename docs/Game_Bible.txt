RESTORIAIDLE
LIVE MULTIPLAYER DEMO GAME BIBLE

Document status: Current source of truth for the live multiplayer demo
Game title: RestoriaIdle
World name: Restoria

PURPOSE
This document defines the playable idle RPG web demo. It contains the current gameplay, content, progression, art, interface, save, social, and data rules needed for the demo. Numerical balance values should remain data-driven unless explicitly fixed here.

The initial single-player offline demo is retired. RestoriaIdle is a live demo: one account carries one character, sign-in is required to play, and the world should feel shared with other players.

======================================================================
1. GAME OVERVIEW
======================================================================

1.1 HIGH CONCEPT
RestoriaIdle is a portrait-oriented 2D idle fantasy RPG set in the open world of Restoria. The player explores a visible world map, selects location-based Activities, and progresses through Combat, Gathering, Standard Production, and Special Production. Other players are visible in the world. The game supports active play and up to 24 hours of unattended progression on the signed-in account.

Core loop:
Explore -> Gather or Fight -> Process -> Create -> Improve -> Explore further

1.2 DESIGN PILLARS
- Location-driven world rather than a menu-only skill list.
- Most locations are available early; danger, inefficiency, equipment, and recipe requirements guide progression.
- Gathering is soft-gated by Proficiency Level.
- Combat is open but dangerous rather than level-gated.
- Production is hard-gated by Proficiency Level and recipe requirements.
- Combat is optional as a deliberate progression path.
- Skills feed into one another through resources, components, equipment, and projects.
- Activities may contain Actions from multiple skills when their equipment requirements are compatible.
- Frequent Actions use controlled reward tables so resources remain meaningful.
- Item and currency values should remain readable and avoid unnecessary inflation.
- Content should be data-driven rather than individually hardcoded.
- The live demo should feel like a multiplayer game: accounts, presence, chat, guilds, shared hubs, and account-owned progress.

1.3 TARGET EXPERIENCE
- Relaxing and cozy during ordinary progression.
- Curious and exploratory when visiting new places.
- Occasionally dangerous without becoming punishing.
- Readable and comfortable to leave open while idling.
- Built around long-term skill and equipment progression.
- Shared: other adventurers, guilds, and hubs are part of ordinary play.

======================================================================
2. CORE TERMINOLOGY
======================================================================

Location Node: A selectable place on a map or sub-map containing Activities and Passive Interactions.

Location: The world place represented by a Location Node. A Location may contain a background, Activities, NPCs, shops, and facilities.

Activity: An ongoing process selected by the player. An Activity repeatedly generates or processes Actions and occupies the single Primary Activity slot.

Action: One resource attempt, creature encounter, combat encounter, or production operation. Each Action defines its skill, category, requirements, timing or combat data, experience, and rewards.

Activity Pool: The weighted list of Actions an Activity can generate at a Location.

Primary Activity: The one ongoing Combat, Gathering, or Standard Production Activity.

Passive Interaction: An interaction that does not interrupt the Primary Activity, such as NPC dialogue, shops, inventory management, or menus at the same Location.

Special Production Project: An instant production operation. The player selects a valid project and quantity, requirements and materials are validated, and the result is granted immediately.

Proficiency Level: The level used for Gathering efficiency and Production requirements.

Base Duration: The normal duration of a non-combat Action before modifiers.

Weight: The relative probability that an Action is selected from an Activity Pool.

Requirement: A mandatory condition for starting or continuing an Activity, Action, recipe, project, or interaction.

Reward: Experience, items, gold, or other progression granted by an Action or project.

Damage Range: The minimum and maximum damage an entity can deal with one attack. Damage is selected as a random integer in the range and cannot be reduced below 1.

Sentence case: Player-facing Display Names use sentence case. Capitalize the first word and proper names only. Example: Copper mine, not Copper Mine Near Town. Existing title-case names may stay until they are edited.

======================================================================
3. ACCOUNT, SAVE, AND UNATTENDED PROGRESSION
======================================================================

3.1 ACCOUNT
- Sign-in is required before play. There is no offline guest path.
- One account carries one character. The character belongs to the account, not to the device.
- Signing in loads that account's save and signs the other device out.
- The first character name is also the account name other players see.
- Character creation chooses a name, a Race, and a starting appearance. Appearance can later be changed from the wardrobe.
- Closed-test builds may also require a tester passkey before sign-in.

3.2 SAVE
- The account save is the source of truth. The client writes it back automatically; there is no sync button.
- A new save is created when the account first creates a character.
- The save contains skills and XP, inventory, bank, equipment, cosmetics, currency, quests, achievements, statistics, settings, current Location, current Activity, Activity start time, unattended-progress timestamp, and save version.
- Saving must support versioning and migration so later demo builds can update existing saves safely.
- Combat and gathering remain client-resolved with soft server validation. Do not make the game remotely authoritative in this demo.

3.3 UNATTENDED PROGRESSION
- Gathering and Combat may continue for up to 24 hours while the game is closed.
- Standard Production queues may contain up to 24 hours of work and continue while the game is closed.
- Special Production completes instantly and does not require unattended simulation.
- On return, elapsed progress is calculated from the saved Activity state, equipment, modifiers, resources, and timestamps.
- Unattended Combat must reproduce the same combat rounds, food use, victories, deaths, rewards, and 30-second death pauses used during active play.

4.1 WORLD MAP
Restoria is presented as a selectable map. Current geography includes:
- A river through the world.
- The Farm, Town, and Goblin Camp near the river.
- Three river crossings.
- Mountains with a cave entrance.
- Wizard's Tower near the mountains.
- Kingswoods between the mountains and Castle.
- River access to the coast and The Docks.
- Temple on the main map.
- The Citadel as a shared hub.

4.2 NAVIGATION
- Main nodes may open Locations or sub-maps containing several Activities and interactions.
- Smaller nodes may contain one Activity or interaction.
- Selecting a node allows travel to that Location.
- A Location shows its Activities, NPCs, shops, and facilities.
- Starting a new Primary Activity replaces the current one.
- Traveling to another Location stops the current Primary Activity immediately.
- Passive Interactions at the active Location do not stop the Primary Activity.

4.3 OPEN-WORLD PROGRESSION
Gathering:
- No hard skill-level access lock.
- Mandatory tools are still required.
- Below the Action's Proficiency Level, Base Duration is doubled.
- Below the Action's Proficiency Level, gathering XP (including bonus skill XP) is halved. Item and gold rewards are unchanged.
- These multipliers live in Config (`gathering_below_proficiency_duration_multiplier`, `gathering_below_proficiency_xp_multiplier`).

Combat:
- No Combat-level access lock.
- Strong enemies discourage early attempts through HP, damage, fight length, and food consumption.

Production:
- Recipes and projects require their defined Proficiency Levels.
- Stations, materials, recipe knowledge, and other requirements may also apply.

Dangerous Areas:
- Remain accessible.
- Clearly communicate danger or Combat chance.
- Can defeat an unprepared player.
- Hostile Activities define a Danger Warning Combat Level.
- When a player travels to a Location while their Combat Level is below that warning, they are immediately forced to start the hostile Combat Activity there.
- If mandatory requirements block that Activity (for example a required tool on a mixed pool), the player still arrives and is warned, but the Activity does not start until requirements are met.

5. ACTIVITY, ACTION, AND POOL SYSTEM
======================================================================

5.1 PRIMARY ACTIVITY
Only one Primary Activity can run at a time:
- Combat.
- Gathering.
- Standard Production.

Starting another Primary Activity stops or replaces the previous one.

5.2 ACTIVITY FLOW
1. Select an Activity at the current Location.
2. Validate Activity requirements.
3. Begin the Activity immediately (no start cooldown).
4. Reference the Activity Pool.
5. Generate one valid Action by weight.
6. Route the Action to its gameplay system.
7. Complete the Action using its timing or Combat rules.
8. Grant rewards and XP.
9. Generate the next Action.
10. Continue until stopped, interrupted, invalidated, or the unattended-progression limit is reached.
11. Primary Activity changes (Start, Stop, Replace, Start queue, and travel) apply immediately. There is no activity-change cooldown. Death pause after defeat still blocks travel and activity changes until recovery finishes.

5.3 MULTI-SKILL POOLS
- An Activity Pool may contain Actions from different skills or gameplay categories.
- XP is granted according to each generated Action, not the parent Activity.
- Mixed pools are allowed only when every possible Action is compatible with the same required equipment setup.

Example Hunting Activity:
- Rabbit: Hunting.
- Pheasant: Hunting.
- Elk: Hunting.
- Wolf: Combat.
- Bear: Combat.

5.4 START VALIDATION
An Activity may start only when the player satisfies the mandatory requirements of every Action that can currently be generated from the pool.

Requirements may include:
- Equipped tool or weapon.
- Production Proficiency Level.
- Station access.
- Recipe knowledge.
- Required item or status.

Activity selection should show required equipment, required levels, Combat chance, and important warnings.

5.5 ACTION DATA
Each Action defines:
- ID and name.
- Category.
- Relevant skill.
- Proficiency Level.
- Base Duration or Combat data.
- Requirements.
- XP.
- Reward table.
- Required tool or weapon.
- Status.

The same Action should use the same core mechanics wherever it appears. Locations determine which Actions appear and their weights.

5.6 WEIGHTED GENERATION AND REWARDS
- Each Activity Pool entry has a weight.
- Weights may total 100 or be normalized.
- Higher-level Locations may contain more advanced Actions or give them greater weight.
- Reward tables are separate from Activity Pools.
- An Action may award nothing.
- Rare rewards should remain rare.

5.7 RECALCULATION
Requirements are re-checked when an Action completes and before the next Action starts, and when the player starts, stops, or replaces an Activity. If a mandatory requirement is no longer met at that boundary, the Activity stops.

An in-progress Action keeps the duration, combat encounter, and held Action computed when that Action was generated. Equipment, skill-level, and buff changes apply to the next Action, not the one already in progress.

======================================================================
7. COMBAT
======================================================================

7.1 COMBAT ACTION
- Combat begins immediately when a Combat Action is generated.
- Combat continues until the enemy or player reaches 0 HP.
- The player attacks first.

7.2 CORE STATS
- Current HP.
- Maximum HP.
- Damage Range.
- Damage Reduction or equivalent armor mitigation.
- Combat Level requirements on equipment.
- Defined buffs and enchantments.
- From Combat Level 10 upward, each Combat Level grants a 1% increase to Maximum HP and Damage Range (Level 10 = +10%, Level 20 = +20%).

7.3 DAMAGE
- Each attack rolls a random integer from the attacker's Damage Range.
- Damage cannot be reduced below 1.

7.4 COMBAT ROUND
1. Player attacks.
2. If the enemy dies, resolve victory.
3. If the enemy survives, it attacks.
4. If the player dies, resolve defeat.
5. Otherwise begin another round.

Combat uses fixed-duration rounds so active and unattended resolution follow the same rules.

7.5 VICTORY
- Award Combat XP.
- Award gold where defined.
- Roll item drops.
- Update kill statistics.
- If HP is below maximum and food is equipped, consume one food and heal by its defined amount.
- Generate the next Action from the selected Activity.

7.6 DEATH
- No XP, item drops, or gold from the failed encounter.
- Pause the Activity for 30 seconds.
- Resume afterward if requirements still permit it.
- Equipment and inventory are not lost.

7.7 FOOD
- Food has a dedicated equipment slot.
- Only one food type is equipped at a time.
- One food is automatically consumed after a victory when HP is below maximum.
- No food is consumed at full HP.

7.8 COMBAT INSIDE OTHER ACTIVITIES
Equipment-compatible non-Combat Activities may generate Combat Actions. After victory, or after the death pause following defeat, the parent Activity continues by generating the next Action. The interrupted Action is not forced to resume.

7.9 WEAPONS AND TOOLS
- Purpose-built weapons are intended for Combat.
- Hunting Net has a Damage Range of 1-1.
- Sling supports basic ranged Combat and Hunting.
- Bows are both Hunting tools and Combat weapons.

9. STANDARD PRODUCTION
======================================================================

9.1 RULES
Standard Production includes Cooking, Metallurgy, Crafting, and Alchemy.

- Repeatable Actions.
- Hard-gated by Proficiency Level.
- Requires the appropriate station.
- Player may browse any Standard Production station and recipes without stopping the current Primary Activity.
- Selecting Start queue validates materials/capacity and starts the queue immediately (replacing any running Primary Activity).
- Completed crafts use the same Reward summary UI as Gathering and Combat (XP, output items, level-ups).
- Queue may contain up to 24 hours of work.
- Mid- and late-game Actions generally take longer than Gathering Actions.
- Longer Actions grant proportionally higher XP.

9.2 COOKING

======================================================================
10. SPECIAL PRODUCTION
======================================================================

10.1 RULES
Special Production includes Smithing, Artisanry, and Arcana.

- Projects are hard-gated by their defined Proficiency Levels and requirements.
- Projects may require NPC knowledge, quests, discoveries, Locations, materials, or gold.
- Materials are validated before completion.
- Projects complete instantly once confirmed.
- Special Production does not use a repeating timer or queue.
- Projects should be material-intensive and grant substantial XP relative to ordinary production.

10.2 SMITHING
Smithing is the instant Special Production skill for conventional metal equipment.

Smithing consumes bars and components created through Metallurgy and Crafting to create metal equipment such as:
- Weapons.
- Armor.
- Shields.
- Metal tools.

Smithing projects complete instantly after all level, material, and project requirements are met.

10.3 ARTISANRY
Artisanry is the master-craftsmanship skill for complex finished equipment and specialist projects.

It combines materials from multiple systems, including:
- Metallurgy materials.
- Crafting components.
- Logs.
- Harvested materials.
- Hunting materials.
- Monster drops.
- Rare resources.
- Gold where defined.

It creates:
- Bows.
- Unique tools.
- Utility items.
- Complex or legendary equipment.

Defined example project: Boar Spear
Requirements:
- Crafting 30.
- Metallurgy 25.
- Artisanry 20.

Materials:
- Steel Spear Head.
- Boar Tusk.
- Oak Shaft.
- Leather Grip.

Output:
- Boar Spear.

10.4 ARCANA
Arcana is the instant Special Production skill for item enchantment and magical production.

Core resources and relationships:
- Essence is a primary Arcana resource gathered through Mining.
- Crafting creates Enchanting Tablets and Spell Tablets.
- Arcana consumes an Enchanting Tablet, Essence, and any project-specific materials to apply an enchantment to a valid item.
- Arcana may also craft prepared Spells (for example Strength Spell) that equip into Spell slots. Equipped spells are always active; they do not cycle.
- Arcana projects may require knowledge from NPCs, quests, discoveries, or Locations.

11. ITEMS, EQUIPMENT, AND INVENTORY
======================================================================

11.1 ITEM PHILOSOPHY
- Item quantities should remain moderate.
- Rare items should remain meaningful.
- Early resources should remain useful in later recipes.
- Equipment should have clear gameplay roles.
- Exact numerical values belong in the game data.

11.2 EQUIPMENT PURPOSES
Tools:
- Enable or improve Gathering Activities.

Weapons:
- Optimize Combat.
- Some also function as Gathering tools.

Armor:
- Improves survivability through defined defensive statistics.

Food:
- Sustains Combat through automatic healing after victories.

Potions:
- Provide Alchemy-created temporary effects only when those effects are explicitly defined in the game data.

11.3 EQUIPMENT SLOTS
Current functional slots:
- Weapon or Tool.
- Shield or Off-hand.
- Helmet.
- Chest.
- Legs.
- Boots.
- Gloves.
- Necklace.
- Ring.
- Back.
- Food.
- Potion.
- Spell 1–4.

Spell slots hold prepared Arcana spells. Exactly one spell may occupy each slot. The same spell type may fill multiple slots. Every equipped spell is always active while it remains in a slot. Duplicate copies stack only when the spell data allows stacking.

11.4 INVENTORY
- The bag has 180 slots. Each item stack (or each enchanted item) uses one slot.
- Non-enchanted stacks may grow to the maximum safe integer; adds stop before overflow.
- Enchanted items never stack.
- Players may sell bag stacks from Inventory: at a Location with an accessible shop that buys the item, they receive that shop's sell price; otherwise they receive 50% of Base Sell Value.
- Standard Production and shops must validate available inventory space before committing.
- Unattended progression grants only what fits and must not overflow slots or stacks.
- Death pause keeps the Primary Activity; clearing/stopping it is refused until recovery ends. Passive browse (recipes, Special Production, shops, NPCs) remains available.

11.5 BANK
- Town Bank and Citadel Bank are dedicated storage Locations.
- The bank uses the same 180-slot stack rules as the bag.
- Gold currency cannot be deposited.
- Banking is a Passive Interaction and does not stop the Primary Activity.

11.6 CHARACTER APPEARANCE
- Race is chosen at character creation and grants defined bonuses and starting items.
- Cosmetic slots and wardrobe items change appearance only.
- Clothes and cosmetics do not grant combat or gathering stats unless a source record explicitly says so.

======================================================================
12. ECONOMY AND SHOPS
======================================================================

12.1 ECONOMY
The economy should avoid:
- Millions or billions of ordinary resources.
- Huge routine stacks.
- Excessively large gold values.
- Guaranteed rewards from every short Action.
- Obsolete early resources.

The economy should encourage:
- Meaningful resource choices.
- Valuable uncommon drops.
- Cross-skill recipes.
- Resource sinks through Production and projects.
- Readable long-term progression.

12.2 ACTION AND REWARD FREQUENCY
- Gathering and Combat Actions are relatively fast.
- Reward probabilities are controlled.
- Some Actions award nothing.
- Rare results remain rare.
- Standard Production becomes slower at higher progression.
- Longer Standard Production grants more XP.
- Special Production is instant but material-intensive.

Illustrative timing ranges:
- Gathering Action: approximately 2-6 seconds.
- Early Standard Production Action: approximately 5-150 seconds.
- Late Standard Production Action: approximately 20-60+ minutes.

12.3 SHOPS
General Store:
- Buys and sells ordinary items.

Mining Store:
- Sells improved pickaxes.
- Pays better prices for ores.
- Requires Mining Level 40.

Wizard's Shop:
- Sells magical items.
- May sell Essence at a premium so Mining remains the primary efficient source.

Additional world shops may be attached to appropriate Locations and NPCs.

15. USER INTERFACE AND WEB LAYOUT
======================================================================

15.1 ORIENTATION
- The game uses a responsive portrait game viewport.
- Important nodes and controls should remain inside a central safe area.
- On narrow screens, the game interface fills the available viewport.
- On wider desktop screens, the portrait game viewport may appear alongside supporting website content.

15.2 TOP HUD
The top HUD should prioritize:
- Total experience.
- Gold.
- Current Activity.
- Current combat or progression information when relevant.

15.3 BOTTOM NAVIGATION
The chin has three sections:
- Current Location (returns to the location screen).
- Character (inventory, equipment, appearance, and related character management).
- Menu nest: Settings, Log, Leaderboards, Guilds.

A chat launcher sits on the location and map screens. Account management is reached from Settings or the social flow after sign-in.

Additional navigation should remain consistent with the same layout.

15.4 INVENTORY SCREEN
Inventory is a full-screen menu with:
- Items.
- Equipment.

Equipment management includes armor, weapon/tool, shield/off-hand, food, potion, and other defined functional slots.

15.5 ACTIVITY SCREEN
An Activity screen may show:
- Location background.
- Current Action.
- Progress or Combat state.
- Required tool.
- Combat chance warning.
- Rewards or recent drops.
- Activity controls.

16. ART DIRECTION
======================================================================

16.1 OVERALL STYLE
A warm, hand-crafted 2D fantasy style that is:
- Inviting.
- Readable.
- Charming.
- Cozy.
- Colorful.
- Timeless rather than realistic.
- Peaceful with occasional danger.

The default mood should not be dark or gritty. Every screen should feel comfortable to leave open for long idle sessions.

16.2 WORLD AND LOCATION ART
- Cozy fantasy environments.
- Distinct biomes and readable landmarks.
- Clear visual separation between safe, ordinary, magical, and dangerous Locations.
- Main-map and Location backgrounds may use square source compositions that crop or scale into the portrait viewport.

16.3 INTERFACE STYLE
- Medium-brown parchment panels.
- Gold highlights.
- Soft green accents.
- Rounded buttons.
- Subtle shadows.
- Large touch-friendly controls.
- Uncluttered mobile-first layout.

16.4 ITEM ICONS
- Bold silhouettes.
- Bright colors.
- Minimal visual noise.
- Consistent proportions.
- Recognizable without reading the item name.

16.5 VISUAL PRINCIPLES
The visual identity should emphasize:
- Cozy fantasy environments.
- Clear equipment and item progression.
- Long-term skilling.
- Distinct biomes.
- Readability over excessive detail.

19. DEMO COMPLETION TARGET
======================================================================

The playable demo should allow the player to:
- Sign in, create one character on the account, and continue that save on any signed-in device.
- See other players, chat, join or found a guild, and use shared Citadel amenities.
- Explore the world map and travel between available Locations.
- Start, stop, and replace Primary Activities.
- Perform weighted Gathering and mixed-category Activities.
- Fight enemies using the defined Combat loop.
- Gain XP and skill levels.
- Gather resources and receive controlled rewards.
- Use equipment, food, inventory, bank, and shops.
- Use Cooking, Metallurgy, Crafting, and Alchemy.
- Complete instant Smithing, Artisanry, and Arcana projects.
- Complete quests and track achievements/statistics.
- Close the game and later receive up to 24 hours of correctly calculated unattended progression on the account save.

======================================================================
20. MULTIPLAYER AND SOCIAL
======================================================================

The live demo should feel shared. These systems are part of ordinary play, not optional overlays hidden until later.

20.1 PRESENCE
- Other signed-in players may appear at Locations and on the map.
- A player's current Location is visible to others unless a privacy setting hides it.

20.2 CHAT
- Chat is available from the location and map screens.
- Public and private chat follow the current moderation and guest-filter rules.

20.3 GUILDS
- Players may create or join a guild.
- A guild has a shared roster, join requests, and one Guild Hall instance.
- The hall has a store house and a debt ledger. Members donate to build it out.

20.4 LEADERBOARDS
- Leaderboards show account-owned characters.
- Rankings update on a defined cadence (daily where implemented).

20.5 BOUNTIES
- Hourly bounty boards are available from the Citadel.
- Turn-in and rewards follow the current bounty catalog and hour window.

20.6 BAZAAR
- The Grand Bazaar lists player-to-player offers.
- Listings are shared, not a copy on one device.

20.7 ARENA
- The Citadel Plaza Arena is optional player combat.
- Search by name or by combat-level ranking.
- Arena fights do not replace the ordinary world Combat loop.

20.8 RESOLUTION
- Combat and gathering stay client-resolved with soft server validation.
- Shared rows (guild roster, hall, bazaar listings, chat, presence) are server-backed when the live backend is configured.
- A build with no backend may keep shared rows on the device so tests and local development still run. That fallback is not a player-facing offline mode.
